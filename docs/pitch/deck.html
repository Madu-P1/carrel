Carrel.

The study workspace that turns your deadlines into a system. Drop in anything you read , PDFs, slides, docs, notes, transcripts , and Carrel plans your week, runs the spaced review, and keeps every answer honest with citations you can click and verify.

I built Carrel because I watched students use eight apps to study for one exam, miss the deadline anyway, and learn nothing they could prove. So I built one app that does the whole loop.

The problem

Studying is eight apps and a calendar that lies.

A second-year med student has 14 lecture PDFs, 6 textbook chapters, 200 highlights in Notion, an Anki deck someone shared on Reddit, three AI tools she pays for, and a final on Friday. None of them know about Friday. None of them tell her what to study tonight. She gets to Thursday at 11pm having studied the wrong material.

The deadline gap

No tool ties what you are reading today to what you owe on Friday. Calendars are passive; study apps are deadline-blind.

The format tax

Lectures are PDFs, slides are PPTX, summaries are Markdown, transcripts are docx, the syllabus is a website. Each tool owns one format and ignores the rest.

The trust gap

Every AI study tool fabricates. A student cannot ship a wrong answer to an exam. Verification has to happen in one click, every time.

02Carrel
The insight

The deadline is the unit of work.
Everything else is in service of it.

Other tools start with the file (Notion), the highlight (Readwise), the question (ChatGPT), or the card (Anki). Carrel starts with Friday. The exam, the paper, the deliverable. From that anchor, every PDF you drop in, every concept you mark weak, every slot of free time on your calendar gets allocated automatically toward closing the gap. The student stops choosing what to study and starts trusting that the system already chose.

03Carrel
The product

Plan first. Everything else feeds the plan.

Plan
Add a deadline. Carrel drafts the study schedule against your real calendar, picks tonight's session, surfaces the weakest concepts, and rebuilds when you fall behind. The home view, every day.
Library
Drop in anything: PDFs, slides (PPTX), Word docs, Markdown notes, Excel sheets, CSV data, plain text. Auto-grouped by subject. The format does not matter; the source does.
Sessions & Review
Spaced-repetition cards generated from your sources. Daily review queue tied to your deadlines, not a generic algorithm. Streak that means something.
Ask & Reader
When you need an answer, ask. Every claim cites the verbatim span; click the chip and Carrel flies you to the source. No fabrication, ever.
