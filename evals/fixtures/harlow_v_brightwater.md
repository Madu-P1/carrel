The plaintiff was injured by a fall in an exterior walkway of a commercial shopping center. The question presented is the scope of the center owner's duty to its invitees.

The owner of a commercial shopping center owes its invitees a duty to maintain the common areas in a reasonably safe condition. This duty extends to walkways, entries, parking structures, and other areas the owner holds open to the general public.

A landowner's knowledge of a recurring hazardous condition may be established by evidence of prior similar incidents. The plaintiff need not prove notice of the precise hazard where a pattern of comparable occurrences would alert a reasonable owner to the danger.

We expressly limit today's holding to the common areas of commercial shopping centers and do not reach the question of liability for hazards arising within individually leased interior tenant spaces. The respective duties of owners and tenants as to leased interiors are governed by their leases and by principles we do not address in this opinion.
