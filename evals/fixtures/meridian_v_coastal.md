This appeal concerns the allocation of premises-liability duties between a property owner and an independent maintenance contractor.

We hold that a maintenance contractor assumes a duty of reasonable care only as to the specific areas and tasks enumerated in its written service agreement. The contractor's undertaking defines the contractor's duty; it does not enlarge that duty by implication. Where a hazard arises in an area the service agreement does not cover, the contractor owes no duty to the injured invitee with respect to that hazard.

It follows that liability for hazards outside the contracted scope remains with the party in possession and control of the premises. The owner cannot delegate away the non-delegable duty it owes its invitees merely by retaining a contractor for limited tasks. Conversely, the owner does not, by retaining such a contractor, become an insurer of that contractor's performance.

We reject the appellant's contention that a property owner is automatically answerable for every act of a retained maintenance contractor. The duty of each party is fixed by its relationship to the hazard and to the premises, not by the existence of the service contract alone. The judgment of the trial court is affirmed.
