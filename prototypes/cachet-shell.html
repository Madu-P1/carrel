Vance v. Meridian Logistics — Opposition to Motion for Summary Judgment
Verification · 4 of 11 statements need your eyes

Argument · Part II.B

The record raises genuine disputes of material fact that preclude summary judgment.

Summary judgment is proper only where the moving party shows that no genuine dispute of material fact remains and that it is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, 477 U.S. 242, 247 (1986). The moving party bears the initial burden of identifying the portions of the record it believes demonstrate the absence of a genuine dispute. Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 323 (1986).

The duty of care extends to all consequences that a reasonable operator would foresee, and "the risk reasonably to be perceived defines the duty to be obeyed in every commercial carriage." Palsgraf v. Long Island R. Co., 248 N.Y. 339, 344 (1928). Defendant's own dispatch logs place the second vehicle within the foreseeable zone of danger.

Where an agency interprets the statute it administers, the reviewing court owes that construction no special weight and decides the question of law for itself. Lochner v. New York, 198 U.S. 45 (1905). The Secretary's reading therefore controls only to the extent it persuades.

A nonmovant need not prove its case at this stage; it need only point to evidence on which a reasonable jury could return a verdict in its favor. Anderson, 477 U.S. at 252. A carrier that assumes control of a hazardous load owes a heightened duty commensurate with the danger. Hollis v. Garrow, 562 U.S. 401, 410 (2011).

Plaintiff's expert concluded that the coupling failed under loads well within its rated capacity, and that the failure was consistent with inadequate inspection. Reyes Dep. 88:14–89:2. That testimony, standing alone, is enough to send the question to a jury.

Because the evidence permits more than one reasonable inference, the dispute is for the factfinder and summary judgment must be denied. Anderson, 477 U.S. at 255.
